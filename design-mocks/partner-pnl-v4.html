How these numbers are computed · click to expand
1. Contract base · monthly ($28.0K)
Sum of dimhome.rent_cost across all installed units this month — the total rent Landing owes the partner at full 100% occupancy. Not a flat per-unit assumption; each unit has its own contracted base rent. Riverside's 20 units:
UnitTypeBase rent
LT subtotal (10 units)$13,000
STR subtotal (10 units)$15,000
Contract base / month$28,000
× 11 months reporting$308,000
2. Actual partner net (LT + STR split)
From the rent-roll signoff sheet (signoff_pulls.json) per month. The net_allocation field is the partner's total cash. We split it into LT vs STR by reservation type: revenue from monthly-billed reservations → LT, revenue from nightly-billed reservations → STR. Sums to the partner's actual total each month.
3. Realistic LT alternative ($261.8K @ 85%)
Contract base × LT occupancy factor = $308.0K × 85% = $261.8K. The 85% factor is a stabilized multi-family standard. Toggle 75% / 95% in the eyebrow for a tighter or looser counterfactual. This is what the partner would have collected if every unit were LT-leased at base rent and ran at typical occupancy.
4. Net delta vs LT-only ($−10.9K @ 85%)
Actual total ($250.9K) − Realistic LT alt ($261.8K) = −$10.9K. Interpretation: the STR program delivered $10.9K LESS over 11mo than a 100%-LT counterfactual at 85% occ. The minus sign means STR is slightly underperforming pure LT — basically a wash at this scale.
5. Recovery % (81%)
Actual total ($250.9K) ÷ Contract base ($308.0K) = 81%. What share of FULL 100%-occupied base rent the partner received. The number a partner will compute on their own — that's why it's the Partner POV headline.
Source mix (49% LT / 51% STR)
From LT ($123.2K) ÷ Actual total ($250.9K) = 49%. From STR ($127.7K) ÷ Actual total ($250.9K) = 51%.

v3 changes vs v2

  • Actual partner-net bar splits into LT (blue, bottom) + STR (coral, top). For mixed-mode properties — most of the portfolio — this surfaces whether LT is carrying the partnership while STR fluctuates, or whether STR is the engine. The shortfall stripes still fill the gap above to the contract-base baseline.
  • Source-mix mini bar in the summary card — single-line LT/STR ratio with embedded percent labels. Read at a glance: is this a mostly-LT property with STR upside, or a STR-heavy property with LT floor support?
  • Breakdown rows show LT and STR contribution explicitly with the source-color dot. The partner can scan the rows and see "the $123K LT is exactly what my contract pays; the $127K STR is what Landing added."
  • Roll-up grid gains LT and STR rows — per-month decomposition, so the operator can point to a specific month and say "that was an STR-strong month" or "that was an LT-only month."
  • Mixed-mode chip in the property strip — split-color square + label "Mixed · 10 LT + 10 STR." Distinguishes from pure-STR and pure-LT properties so operators know which framing applies before they read the chart.
  • Pure-STR properties (One Club, Compass) show 100% STR — the source-mix bar collapses to one color, and the LT breakdown row simply doesn't render. The view degrades gracefully for these cases.
  • Riverside Lofts placed first because the decomposition is the v3 motivating feature. Pure-STR examples follow to confirm the view works at the edge case.

Implementation requirements

  • Backend needs to split net_allocation per month into actual_lt and actual_str. From reservations: sum revenue contribution by rent_unit (night vs month) for each month in the reporting window, then apportion partner's share by take rate.
  • Alternatively, group source units by str_status: revenue from str-enabled units = STR portion, revenue from non-STR units = LT portion. Cleaner accounting if Landing tracks unit-level revenue allocation.
  • For the rent roll data we already have, the simplest approximation: split partner revenue by ratio of (STR reservation nights × ADR) to (LT reservation rent × occupied days). This is a derivation, not a primary field — flag in the tooltip that "STR/LT mix is estimated from reservation-level data."
