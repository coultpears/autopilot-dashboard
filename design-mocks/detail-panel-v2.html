Design rationale

  • Trend module above the tabs, not inside one. Operators answer "is this property earning?" before drilling into units. Putting the trend in a tab would bury it; making it the second thing after the stat band lets it carry the panel.
  • Stacked bars: Landing (solid, bottom) + Partner (soft, top). The first iteration showed only monthly_net_allocation — which is actually partner's cut, not Landing's. Splitting the bar into Landing's landing_margin and partner's net_allocation shows the real economic split. Total bar height = gross rent. The take-rate jumps out: 30% on a typical LT deal (Brookwood) vs 61% on a Landing-favorable STR deal (One Club).
  • Split summary card. Two columns — Landing | Partner — with take % as the headline. Operators evaluating expansion candidates can compare contract economics at a glance.
  • Coral for STR, blue for LT. The two product lines have different economics and different optimization levers. Color-coding makes mixed-mode portfolios scannable in the grid.
  • "S" mark on the Unit, not a column. Adding a column at 880px breaks the table. The 16px coral square inside the unit cell is loud enough to scan while costing zero layout.
  • Three-line rent stack for STR units. Nightly rate (truth), stay total (felt impact, e.g. "$488 stay total (4nt)"), normalized monthly (legacy — struck through). Operators currently read the monthly figure as if it were comparable to LT; the strike-through breaks that habit without removing the data.
  • Ghost bars for pre-onboarding months. Empty slots would let the eye read a 4-month series as a complete picture. Dashed outlines say "Landing wasn't here yet" so the ramp reads correctly.
  • Markup gets a "STR-est" tag when nightly. The formula is different (nightly × expected occupied nights vs base) and the result has wider variance; the tag warns the reader not to read it like a fixed monthly markup.
  • "Daily Rev" → "Today $", repositioned next to Landing Rent. Operators were unsure what the column meant. New header tooltip explains it; column moved so the three rent metrics (Base / Landing / Today) sit together. Markup follows as the trailing economic indicator.
  • STR-eligible-but-dormant units (e.g. unit 1501) get a distinct visual. The "S" mark goes from filled to hollow-dashed; an "STR paused" tag appears next to the Mid-Term badge; the rent stack shows the property's ADR as "~$140/nt avail" to surface missed nightly upside. Markup column splits realized (+29.5% monthly) vs STR potential (+82%) so operators spot under-monetized units.
  • STR markup column = monthly truth, not per-night flattery. First version computed "+106%" by comparing the nightly rate's monthly equivalent to monthly base — mathematically correct for per-night unit economics, but misleading because it ignored occupancy. A unit booked 4 nights at $122/nt earns $488 against $1,800 of base rent owed for the full month — actually −73% margin, not +106%. The column now leads with MTD realized margin (can be negative), then shows the break-even nights/occupancy threshold so operators know the floor, then a small "per stay" rate-quality number. Unit 1103 (15nt @ $206 = +58% MTD) is the only profitable STR this month — operators see the pattern instantly.
  • Added stat: "Occ today". The trend ends at month-close; operators want today's number alongside the trend.
  • Added stat (STR only): "ADR · 30d". Nightly properties get a 6th stat slot that LT properties don't need. Stats are flex-wrap, so this scales.
  • "Revenue history" as a third tab is wired up but inert here — a place to drop drill-down (per-unit revenue, channel mix, comparable-property benchmark) without crowding the inline trend.
  • What I did NOT change. The Units table column order, the badge vocabulary (curr/vacant/future/other), the unit-dot palette, the deinstall column, and the Reservations tab structure. Existing muscle memory is preserved.
