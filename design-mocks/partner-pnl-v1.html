Design rationale

  • Implied as ghost bar / actual as solid fill / gap as diagonal-stripe shortfall — the core visual. The empty space at the top of each bar IS the missing money. Diagonal coral stripes give it texture without screaming. Eye reads "did we fill the bar?" in one glance.
  • Cumulative shortfall is the headline number, displayed at large mono weight in the summary card. For an operator deciding whether to defend an STR deployment to a partner, this is THE number that matters. $58K shortfall > 19% recovery > the per-month chart. Stack-ranked left to right by what hits hardest.
  • Two reference lines on the chart: implied @ 100% (dashed dark) and realistic @ 85% LT occ (dashed amber). Operators can mentally compare against both — "we missed full base by $58K" but also "we missed realistic LT by $47K." The realistic line is the harder benchmark and the more honest comparison.
  • Toggle in the eyebrow lets the reader swap the primary benchmark (implied vs realistic). For the mockup both lines are always drawn, but the toggle controls which is the headline benchmark in the verdict card.
  • Per-month roll-up grid below the chart — scannable, exact numbers per month + recovery %. Backstop for the bar chart, useful when comparing two close months.
  • Trajectory glyph reflects month-over-month direction of the gap, not direction of revenue. "Gap widened" (▾, red), "Gap closing" (▴, green), "Holding" (→, ink-2), "Surplus trending" (▴, green). Operators care whether things are getting better or worse from a partner-fairness standpoint.
  • Narrative line at the bottom — one italic sentence translating the chart into language an operator could use in a partner call. Required: name the average monthly gap in dollars, call out the trend, suggest the next signal to watch.
  • Contrast example shows what success looks like. The Compass surplus case has the verdict color flip to neutral/green and the trajectory glyph flip up. Important so operators don't read "everything is doom" — the view's job is to surface BOTH conditions.
  • What this view does NOT do (intentional restraint): it doesn't suggest action ("convert to LT") or compute hypothetical scenarios. The job is "make the reality visible." Operator decisions belong elsewhere.
