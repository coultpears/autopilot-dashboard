v2 changes vs v1

  • Perspective toggle (Partner POV / Landing POV) added to the eyebrow. Default is Partner POV — what the partner will see when they pull the data themselves. Operator clicks Landing POV before a partner call to load the rebuttal framing. The toggle button itself is color-coded: red border when Partner POV active (because that view will show the partner's complaint), blue border when Landing POV (the call-prep view).
  • Landing POV reframes the SAME numbers: headline becomes "Incremental $ delivered" (revenue the partner has thanks to Landing); breakdown adds "Without Landing · LT alt" and "Vacancy captured" rows; trajectory glyph and narrative shift to operator-friendly language.
  • "Vacancy captured" metric — % of available unit-nights with revenue. Strong call-prep number: even when $ is below LT alt, a high vacancy-capture % shows Landing is moving the building toward stabilized utilization.
  • Talking points block (Landing POV only) — pre-written sentences for the partner call. Each bullet uses the actual numbers from the data. Saves the operator from doing live math in front of the partner.
  • LT-occupancy benchmark toggle (75 / 85 / 95) — lets operators adjust the realistic LT assumption per property. Urban properties may justify 95%; resort/seasonal can argue 75%. The chart's realistic-LT line, the summary's "Realistic LT" row, and the Landing POV verdict all recompute live.
  • Both perspectives are in the DOM simultaneously — toggle just swaps CSS visibility. Means operator can click between them instantly with no re-fetch, no animation lag. Critical for call mid-flight.
  • v1 file preserved at partner-pnl-v1.html for direct A/B comparison — open both side by side to see the framing difference.

What the operator does with this

  • Before a partner call: switch to Landing POV, adjust LT% to the assumption you want to defend, copy the talking points.
  • During a partner call: keep Partner POV visible (what they're seeing) and have Landing POV in another tab; switch when you need a counter.
  • For internal review: Partner POV is the "what does this look like from the outside" gut-check; Landing POV is "what story can we tell."
